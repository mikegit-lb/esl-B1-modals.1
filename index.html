Module 01 / Structure & meaning

Make the modal do the work.

Modal verbs show attitude: ability, permission, duty, possibility, and conclusions. Explore the four families, then test your choices.

The fast rule

After most modals, use the base verb: modal + verb.

Can describes present ability or informal permission. Could is useful for past ability and polite requests. May is more formal permission.

Could I borrow your notes?

Must often expresses the speaker's strong rule. Have to usually points to an external rule. Should and ought to give advice.

You must wear a helmet here.

Might shows a weak possibility. Must shows a confident conclusion. Can't says an idea is impossible.

The lights are on, so Maya must be home.

Use modal + have + V3 to talk about an earlier possibility, regret, or deduction.

You should have told me earlier.

Remember the shape.

A modal does not take to before the next verb, except in phrases such as ought to and have to.

  • must leave
  • might rain
